Notes on facts
- Star WD J1330+6435, white dwarf, atmosphere enriched with calcium, lithium, sodium from rocky planetary debris; ~4,000 °C (not used); "chartreuse green" in the team's models; first known genuinely green star per the researchers. Warwick press release / EurekAlert / Phys.org (Oct 16).
- Mechanism: dense sodium broadens absorption across yellows, oranges and some reds (Reuters via Detroit News; Nature World News quoting Hollands).
- Colour calculated from measured starlight plus a model of human colour vision; nobody has seen it green through an eyepiece (Reuters; Sky at Night).
- Distance deliberately omitted: outlets disagree (≈280 vs ≈326 light-years).
- Pink/carbon white dwarf WD J1011+2845, "may be the reddest star ever observed" (Live Science, Sci.News, PopSci). ~1 in 30 white dwarfs unusual colour (CNN; Sci.News census of 151 nearby white dwarfs).
- Sun's spectrum peaks in blue-green yet looks white; no blackbody temperature looks green (Discover, BBC Science Focus, Mental Floss).
- Study: Hollands et al., RAS Techniques and Instruments.
